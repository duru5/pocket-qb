How it plays
  1. Huddle. The 40-second clock is running. You only see the defense's personnel. Pick a call and a kill (the play you can switch to at the line).
  2. Line. The defense walks up, shows its tells and may rotate late. Tap the coverage you see: a right read gets the ball out 70 ms sooner, the wrong shell costs 80 ms of hesitation. Watch for blitzers walking down and slide the line toward them, or keep the back in. Tap the call card to kill, then snap. Snapping early beats a late rotation; waiting shows you more.
  3. Live. Hold anywhere to move your eyes (defenders with instincts follow them). Tap a receiver for a fast ball, double-tap for touch. The ring around the QB is the pocket closing; a red rusher is unblocked, so get it out hot. After the catch the receiver runs: a ball to a man in space is worth more than the air yards say.
  4. Result. No dice on the throw: it is caught unless a defender can get to it. Inside 1 yard at the catch point he breaks it up (or picks it if he was there first, in front); inside 2 yards it is a contested catch with no yards after; a bullet through a defender's lane gets tipped. The result names the defender and the distance, shows the best throw available when you released and the best window of the rep, and what the defense played.
  5. Personnel and runs. Pick 11, 12, 21 or 10 personnel in the huddle; the defense subs to match. Heavy sets bring base defense and a crowded box, spread sets bring nickel and dime. Runs (green) are judged by the box count; play-action (gold) pulls linebackers a step or two downhill before they drop, which opens the intermediate windows behind them.
  6. Game modes. Drive, two-minute drill, a fourth quarter or a second half. The game clock runs between snaps unless the last play stopped it; use timeouts and the spike late. On fourth down choose to go, punt or kick. The defensive coordinator adapts: run a lot and he loads the box, beat a coverage and he calls it less.
